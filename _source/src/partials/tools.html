How to use these calculators

Compound interest shows how contributions and returns stack over time. The gap between the grey area (what you put in) and the blue area (growth) is the reward for starting early. Read how to invest your first $1,000.

Retirement / FIRE uses a safe-withdrawal rate: divide the spending your savings must cover by the rate (4% means 25× spending). Enter expected pension, CPP/OAS or Social Security income to reduce the target. See how much you need to retire.

Mortgage and Loan / EMI use standard amortization. Early payments are mostly interest, so extra payments in the first years save the most. Try the extra-payment field, then read pay down the mortgage or invest?

Fee impact compares two annual fees on the same portfolio. Small differences compound into large sums; see the true cost of a 1% fee. Dividend income projects cash flow with optional reinvestment. Position size caps the loss on any trade at a fixed percentage of your account. Inflation shows how much buying power cash loses each year.

All results are estimates based on the inputs you choose and constant rates of return, which real markets never deliver. They are for education only and are not financial advice.
