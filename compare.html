How to choose the right investing account

Answer three questions to see which type of provider fits you, then use our 7-point checklist to compare any brokerage, robo-advisor or advisor on the things that matter.

The 7-point checklist

  1. Protection. In the US, check the broker is a SIPC member (securities up to $500,000, including $250,000 cash, if the firm fails). In Canada, check CIPF membership. Neither protects against market losses.
  2. All-in cost. Commissions, account fees, fund expense ratios, currency conversion, transfer-out and inactivity fees. Use the fee impact calculator.
  3. Account types. TFSA/RRSP/FHSA in Canada or IRA/Roth IRA and 401(k) rollovers in the US, plus joint and corporate accounts if you need them.
  4. Investments offered. Stocks, ETFs, bonds, GICs/CDs, options, international markets, fractional shares and automatic recurring purchases.
  5. Tools and research. Screeners, charts, analyst research and tax reporting quality.
  6. Service. Phone support hours, response times and how easy it is to transfer accounts in and out.
  7. Track record. Regulatory history (FINRA BrokerCheck, CIRO), outages and complaints.

Choosing a broker: FAQ

Are $0-commission brokers really free?

Stock and ETF trades often are, but brokers still earn money from order flow, interest on cash balances, margin lending, options contracts, currency conversion and premium tiers. Read the fee schedule for the things you'll actually do.

Is my money protected if a broker fails?

In the US, SIPC covers securities up to $500,000 (including $250,000 cash) per customer per capacity. In Canada, CIPF provides coverage with separate limits by account category. Neither covers investment losses.

Which account should I open first?

Usually a tax-advantaged account (Roth IRA or 401(k) in the US; TFSA or RRSP in Canada) before a taxable account. See our TFSA vs RRSP and Roth vs Traditional IRA guides.
